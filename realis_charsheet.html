Step 1 — Pick a Class

Elodie picks Xenagogue.

The Xenagogue is a showy tour guide and courier from a long legacy of navigators and messengers — someone who always knows the way through, and always makes an entrance.

Elodie wanted to play a pilot. Someone who moves between moons for a living, has a ship with a name, and announces themselves before they've even docked. Xenagogue in the Sphere of Sojourn fit immediately.

Sphere of Sojourn — Impulse
Respond to a local problem with excursionary Means. Elodie's Token recharges whenever Maren solves something by going somewhere, finding a route, or invoking her history as a traveler — win or lose.
Elodie's reasoning
"I like that the Impulse recharges my Token for doing exactly what the character would do anyway. I'm not playing against my instincts to get a resource back."

Step 2 — Name and Basics

The character has a name.

Name, pronouns, a line or two of context. Elodie keeps it short — Realis characters reveal themselves through play, not backstory documents.

Maren
she/her · Xenagogue · Sphere of Sojourn

Elodie's reasoning
"Two sentences. She has a history, a specific route, and already a hint of something morally complicated. That's enough to start."

Before the sheet

What is a Sentence?

A Sentence is a bold statement of what your character can always do. Your Class gives you four. They're the only mechanic that matters — when you want something uncertain to happen, you name a Sentence and use it.

Reality Rank (+0 to +3)
Every Sentence has a rank. Higher rank wins in a Conflict. All four start at +0. Ties go to whoever is defending — so at +0, you lose every tie as the one acting. This is by design. Losing is how you grow.
Countered
Lose a Conflict with a Sentence and it's Countered for the rest of the scene. Cross it out — you can't use it again until the scene changes. That's also a mark on the Sentence.
Primed → Realization
Three marks = Primed. You can then Realize it: rank goes up by 1, and you add a limiting condition to the text. The Sentence becomes stronger and more specific to your story.

Step 4 — Hallmarks

Naming the ship.

Hallmark
Words wrapped in ❧these marks☙ in a Sentence are Hallmarks — placeholders for something specific to your character. Give each one a concrete name. It's free worldbuilding and it makes the Sentence yours.
Orphan Vessel
A ship or vehicle without a registered owner — salvaged, stolen, or inherited. Several Sojourn classes have one. Give it a name and a rough shape. No stats needed.

Maren's first Sentence reads: I always pilot my ❧Orphan Vessel☙ with great skill.

Elodie names the Hallmark
❧Orphan Vessel☙ → the Pale Chord
A mid-range courier vessel, wide hull, looks slower than it is. The name came with the ship. Maren hasn't changed it.

The Sentence now reads: I always pilot the Pale Chord with great skill. Same mechanic, but it's a real thing in the fiction now.

Step 5 — Dream

Maren's Dream.

Dream
One Sentence. Single use, ever. Cross it off and you overcome any uncertain obstacle — even one you'd normally lose. The Xenagogue's examples are in the book. Pick one, rework it, or write your own. Once used, it's gone.

Elodie looked at the three examples and rewrote one to fit what she already knows about Maren.

Maren's Dream
I will find out what was in that last cargo — and make sure it never moves again.
Elodie is keeping this private for now. Maren doesn't consciously know this is where she's headed. But Elodie does.
Elodie's reasoning
"It ties directly to her backstory and gives me a compass for the whole campaign. I know roughly what the arc looks like, even if Maren doesn't."

Step 7 — Bonds

What are Bonds?

Bond
A Sentence about your relationship with another player character. Use it as a Means in a Conflict when the fiction reflects it. Two Bonds, two different people. Every use marks it. Three marks and it Realizes — both named characters recharge Tokens.

Bonds can't be written until other characters are posted. Elodie waits until she can read the other sheets, then writes toward specific people — not placeholders.

Too vague
Cassiel and I go way back.
No point of view. Useless at the table.
Good
Cassiel knows things about the Outer Shelf I don't — which means I need them closer than I'm comfortable with.
Specific, active, and tells you exactly when the Bond is relevant.

Reference — Terms you'll see on sheets

Words that need explaining.

The Drift
The strange space between the moons. Not quite real. Some classes can navigate it; most treat it as a hazard. If your Class mentions the Drift, ask the GM what it looks like in this campaign — it varies by table.
Orphan Vessel
A ship or vehicle without a registered owner — salvaged, stolen, or inherited. Several classes have one as a Hallmark. Name it, give it a rough shape. No stats needed.
Ephemera
Objects and resources your character carries that are useful but limited — vials, forged papers, calling cards. Unlike Sentences, Ephemera can be used up, lost, or traded. Your Class describes what kinds of things your character tends to have.
Moon Sentence
A Sentence that's true about the current location — established by the GM or by a player spending a Token. Spend your Token to invoke one as a Means in a Conflict.
Honing
Once per session: mark a Class Sentence, write a temporary condition in parentheses, bump its rank +1 for the session. Removed at session end. Can't Hone a Primed Sentence.
