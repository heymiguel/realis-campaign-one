Making your first character.

First things first

No dice. No stats. Just statements.

Realis is a collaborative storytelling game set across a thousand strange moons — science-fantasy, sword and sorcery, space opera. It's diceless. Your character is defined by a set of bold statements called Sentences. When two characters want opposite things, you compare whose Sentence is more Real. Higher rank wins.

What it feels like at the table

Mostly conversation. The GM describes what's happening, players say what they do, the group figures out what happens next. Conflict resolution kicks in when the outcome is uncertain and opposed — not for everything.

The thing that surprises people

You will lose your first conflicts. That's intentional. Losing is how your character grows. Get a Sentence Countered three times and it gets more powerful — but also more specific to your story.

The core mechanic

Sentences are what your character can always do.

Your character has four Sentences that come with their Class. They all start at +0. When one fails in Conflict three times, you can Realize it: the rank goes up by 1 and you add a limitation to the text. It becomes stronger, and more specific to your character's actual story.

+0
I always wear the right outfit.
Broad. Works almost anywhere. But you lose on ties at +0.
+1
I always wear the right outfit during important events.
After three failures. Stronger, but random taverns don't count.
+2
I always wear the right outfit during important events which I was invited to.
Crashes don't count now either.
+3
I always wear the right outfit during important events which a friend invited me to.
Maximum rank. This Sentence has a story now.
When things get uncertain

How Conflict works.

If you're doing something unopposed or where the outcome isn't interesting, you just succeed — describe it and move on. Conflict kicks in when the outcome is uncertain and opposed.

1
Declare Intent and Means
Say what you want to happen — dramatically, not just physically. "I want to humiliate him in front of his crew" is better than "I punch him." Then name which Sentence you're using to do it.
2
Counteractor declares their Means
If someone is opposing you, they name their Sentence. No opposition? You succeed automatically.
3
Compare ranks. Higher wins. Ties go to the defender.
You can spend your Token before comparing to boost your Sentence by +1.
You win Your intent comes to pass. Describe what happens.
They win Your Sentence is Countered. Put parentheses around it — you can't use it again until the scene changes. Mark it. Three marks and it's ready to Realize.
Your character sheet

Six things. That's the whole sheet.

Class Sentences ×4

What you can always do. Come with your Class. All start at +0. The fourth one is a flaw — it can be used against you, but deliberately triggering it can recharge your Token.

Bonds ×2

Sentences about your relationship with another PC. Can be used as a Means in Conflict. Each Bond must target a different person. Three uses and it Realizes — both characters recharge Tokens.

Dream ×1

Single use, ever. Cross it off and you overcome any uncertain obstacle. Save it for a moment that defines your character. Achieving your Dream is a natural way to retire.

Token ×1

Rechargeable. Spend it to boost any Means +1 for one Conflict. Recharges when you act on your Sphere's Impulse during a Conflict, or when a Bond Realizes.

Impulse

Your Sphere's recurring instinct. Act on it during a Conflict — win or lose — and your Token recharges. You'll write this on your sheet from your Sphere's entry.

Band Sentence (shared)

One Sentence the whole group shares, starting at +1. Used once per session when everyone agrees. Written together in Session Zero: "When we work together, we always…"

Step 1 of character creation

Pick a Class.

There are 50+ classes organized into ten Spheres. A Sphere is a cluster of classes that share a common instinct — their Impulse. Don't optimize. Ask yourself: when things go wrong, what does your character reach for first? That'll point you to a Sphere.

You can switch classes mid-campaign. If a class stops feeling right after a few sessions, frame a scene where your current character steps out of the action — then introduce someone new. There's no penalty.

The next slide has every Sphere with all its classes and their Sentences. Click a Sphere on the left to open it.

One note on the Sphere of Semantics: those classes have extra mechanical complexity — one of them has you manage two character sheets at once. Worth avoiding for your first character unless that specifically appeals to you.

Step 2 of character creation

Copy your four Class Sentences.

Once you've picked a class, write its four Sentences on your sheet. They all start at +0. Leave room next to each for marks.

The first three Sentences are your main tools — your skills, equipment, and areas of mastery. They're intentionally broad at first.

+0
I always get the drop on my target.
+0
I always coat my blade with a useful poison.
+0
I always disappear into the dark.

The fourth Sentence is a flaw, quirk, or hindrance. It can be used against you — but you can also trigger it deliberately to recharge your Token (called a Tragic Success).

+0
I always find a well-paying job.

The flaw above makes the Blackguard mercenary by nature — useful for the GM to create complications, but also something the player can lean into for Token economy.

Step 2 continued

Name your Hallmarks.

Some Sentences have words wrapped in special marks: ❧like this☙. Those are Hallmarks. They're placeholders for something specific to your character — a weapon, a mount, a companion, a vessel. Name them now.

Before
I always wield a ❧beautiful and distinct weapon☙.
↓ you name the hallmark
After
I always wield Vespertine, my crescent-bladed sword.
Now the Sentence is yours, not generic. It does the same thing mechanically — it just has a name now.
Before
I always hunt alongside my ❧loyal companion☙.
↓ you name the hallmark
After
I always hunt alongside Thresh, my grey hunting hound.
The companion has a name. That matters when the story has to reckon with them.
Keep Hallmarks broad at first. A Sentence like "I always have the necessary tool on hand" is intentionally vague — its power is in the flexibility. Don't limit it in your head before Realization does it for you.
Step 3 of character creation

Choose your two Bonds.

Bonds are Sentences about your relationship with another player character. They can be used as a Means in Conflict — but only when the fiction reflects that relationship being relevant. Each Bond targets a different person. Your two Bonds can't go to the same character.

Your Class comes with example Bonds. Use them, rework them, or write your own. Good Bonds are specific and active.

Too vague
[Character] and I have been through a lot together.
This doesn't give you anything to grab onto in play.
Too vague
I care about [Character].
Still not actionable.
Good
[Character] is a wild bull — but sometimes you need a wild bull.
Specific. Tells you how this character sees the other one. Has a point of view.
Good
I will prove I am stronger than [Character], even if that means fighting them myself.
Active. Generates story. You know exactly when this Bond is relevant.
Bonds in play

Using and growing your Bonds.

Using a Bond as a Means

Use it like any other Sentence. But the fiction has to reflect the Bond — the other character is present, or their presence is clearly felt, or the thematic link is obvious. It can't just be "I use my Bond."

Marks and Realization

Bonds get marked after every use, win or lose. Three marks and the Bond is Primed. When it Realizes, both characters named in it recharge their Tokens. Then the Bond evolves, same as a Class Sentence.

Boosting with Tokens

You or the other named character can each spend a Token to boost the Bond +1 before it's compared. If both of you spend, it goes up +2. Useful when the Bond is your best option.

As long as a Bond is still +0 and has no marks, you can erase it and rewrite it completely. Once it's been Realized, it stays on its path.
Step 4 of character creation

Pick your Dream.

A Dream is a single-use, once-ever Sentence. Cross it off and you overcome any uncertain obstacle — even one you'd normally lose. Your Class has three example Dreams. Pick one, or write your own.

Your character can know their Dream as a conscious goal, or it can be something you as a player are steering toward privately. Both work.

Berserker
I will find transcendent clarity in the midst of battle and become my truest self.
Questionist
I will solve the case that everyone told me was impossible.
Oriser
I will complete the pilgrimage and return home changed.
Duelist
I will defeat my rival in fair and final combat.
Achieving your Dream is a natural place to retire your character. But you don't have to — some characters are more interesting to follow after they've gotten what they wanted.
Step 5 of character creation

Write your Impulse. Take your Token.

Copy your Sphere's Impulse onto your character sheet. This is how your Token recharges during play.

Impulse example — Sphere of Shadow

Respond to a noble problem with dishonorable Means.

If you're in the Shadow Sphere, your Token recharges whenever you use a Means that fits this pattern during a Conflict — win or lose.

Also recharges your Token

Triggering your flaw Sentence on purpose, leading yourself into trouble (Tragic Success). And when one of your Bonds Realizes, both named characters recharge.

Spending your Token

Boost a Means +1 for the current Conflict. Do this before comparing ranks.

Invoke a Moon Sentence — if a Moon Sentence has been established for the area you're in, you can use it as a Means.

Add a new Moon Sentence — write a new +0 Sentence onto the current Moon Sheet describing something true about this place. You can use it as a Means immediately.

Step 6 of character creation

Name your character.

Name, pronouns, and a Sentence or two of backstory. That's all you need. Don't over-explain — your character will reveal themselves through play.

What to include

Name and pronouns.

What they look like, roughly.

A sentence or two about who they are before the story begins.

Any pre-existing connections to other player characters — things they've already done together, old debts, shared history.

What to leave open

Most of your past. Realis characters are shaped by what happens to them during play, not before it.

Family, backstory trauma, long origin histories — these can be there if you want them, but they're not load-bearing.

Leave room for the story to surprise you about who this person actually is.

Your four Class Sentences already tell us a lot about who your character is. The backstory is just context for those Sentences — not the other way around.
Session Zero · Before anything else

Foundation Sentences.

Before characters are finalized and before play starts, the group writes Foundation Sentences together. These are your shared safety tools — anyone can invoke them at any time to pause play and redirect the story.

You fill in three prompts, writing as many sentences as you need for each:

Our story always…
rewards curiosity over caution.
makes consequences feel real.
What you want the story to consistently do.
Our story never…
uses sexual violence as a plot device.
treats death as comedy.
Hard limits. Non-negotiable, always in effect.
Our story will, but only with distance and care, include…
loss of a character the group loves.
betrayal within the Band.
Things that are allowed but need a heads-up and some care in the handling.
Session Zero · Your group

Create your Band.

A Band is the group of characters playing together. It has three things: a Concept, a Name, and a Band Sentence.

Band Concept

A short phrase for what ties the group together — the kind of work they do, a shared relationship, or some metaphysical link. Start small and focused.

A research team digging into the mysteries of a dead moon. A circus trying to earn enough to escape a backwater world.

Band Name

What your characters call themselves. Can be grand and self-important, or something they got stuck with. Doesn't have to mean anything deep.

The Harrock Expedition. Mr. Besto's Silver Hoop Parade. Ratrunners, Inc. Band of the Charred Axe.

Band Sentence

Always starts at +1. Always begins: "When we work together, we always…" One use per session, requires full group agreement to invoke.

"When we work together, we always find justice on the end of a blade."

You can rewrite the Band Sentence at any time — start or end of session, even mid-play — as long as everyone agrees. The rank stays the same. The Band is a living thing.
