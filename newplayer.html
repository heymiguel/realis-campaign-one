The basics

What is a tabletop RPG?

A tabletop RPG is a structured group storytelling game. One person — the GM — sets the scene and plays the world. Everyone else plays a single character who lives in that world. Together, you figure out what happens.

There's no board. No screen. No winning or losing. You're making a story together, with rules that give the story shape and stakes.

What the GM does

Describes the world, plays the people your characters meet, and moves the story forward. Not an opponent — more like a narrator who's also playing.

What you do

Play one character. Say what they do, how they react, what they say. You don't control the world — just your character.

In practice

What does it actually feel like?

Mostly conversation. The GM describes a situation. You say what your character does. The GM describes what happens next. Other players do the same. It builds.

A typical exchange
GM: "The dockmaster won't look at you. He's been paid off — you can tell by the way he's holding his hands."

Maren's player: "I slide the Pale Chord's registry papers across the counter and say, loudly enough for the people behind me to hear: 'I've made this run forty-three times. You've stamped these papers forty-three times. Let's not make forty-four memorable.'"

GM: "He stamps them. Doesn't look up."

No dice. No apps. Just people deciding what happens.

Your job

What's expected of you as a player?

Play your character

Say what they do and how they feel. You don't have to be a great actor or writer. A sentence is enough. "Maren hangs back and watches the door" is a complete contribution.

Engage with the others

The story is better when characters interact. React to what other players do. Let their choices affect yours. You're not playing solo — you're playing together.

Show up

This is an async game — no fixed session time. But the game only moves when people post. Aim for a reply every few days. If you need to step back, just say so.

You don't need to
Know the rules inside out. Have a fully formed character backstory. Be funny or dramatic. Play a hero. Stay in character 100% of the time.

About Realis specifically

This game has no dice.

Most RPGs use dice to decide whether things succeed or fail. Realis doesn't. Instead, your character has a set of bold statements — called Sentences — that describe what they can always do.

When two characters want opposite things, you compare whose statement is stronger. Higher rank wins. That's the whole mechanic.

A Sentence
I always find the way through or around.

If Maren uses that Sentence to navigate past a blockade, and nobody's opposing her, she just does it. If someone is opposing her, we compare their Sentences and the stronger one wins.

Your Sentences start broad and get more specific as the campaign goes on. Failing actually makes them stronger. More on that in the Player Guide.

How we're playing it

This is a play by post game.

We're not meeting at a table or on a call. We're playing through Discord, in writing, on our own schedules. The GM posts a scene. Players write their characters' responses when they can. The story moves in pieces, over days and weeks.

What's good about it

No scheduling. No one has to be online at the same time. You can take your time with what you write. The story stays at a pace that works for everyone.

What to watch for

It's easy to drift — to go quiet without meaning to. The game only moves when people post. A few lines every few days keeps it alive. If you need a break, just say so.
